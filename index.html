00 / THESIS

Timewarp is not only an attack primitive. It is also an already-valid, miner-controlled way to alter Bitcoin's block cadence. Before removing that option, Bitcoin should measure its full design space.
THE CLAIM WE CAN DEFEND

Not “Satoshi planned it.” Not “there is no risk.” The serious claim is that a real protocol option is being closed before its constructive use has been rigorously tested.

01 / INTRODUCTION

First: what does
Bitcoin call “time”?

Bitcoin does not have one authoritative clock. It has several measurements that normally move together closely enough that users rarely notice the difference.

01

Wall time

The time humans and network nodes experience. Miners cannot make a real week pass twice as fast.

02

Block height

A count of accepted blocks. Subsidy halvings and height-based deadlines follow this clock.

03

Header timestamp

A value selected by the miner who creates each block. It is constrained, but it does not need to equal wall time.

04

Median Time Past

The median of eleven recent timestamps. Bitcoin uses it for time-based transaction locks.

timestamps, not to time.

The implementation actually measures 2,015 intervals, a historic off-by-one preserved by consensus.

Bitcoin adjusts mining difficulty every 2,016 blocks. If the period appears to take longer than expected, the next period becomes easier. If it appears shorter, mining becomes harder.

The important detail is that Bitcoin measures the period using timestamps written into the first and final blocks—not an external clock.

02 / THE MECHANISM

How can seven days
look like fourteen?

The trick is not to speed up physical time. It is to create a larger timestamp span than the real time miners spent producing the blocks.

STEP 01

Keep ordinary timestamps low

timestamp = MTP + 1

Participating miners choose the smallest timestamp allowed by the previous eleven blocks.

STEP 02

Jump at the period's end

observed span ≈ 14 days

The final participant chooses a higher timestamp, making the period appear longer than it was.

STEP 03

Reset and repeat

next first block → low again

Current rules allow the next period's first timestamp to fall back toward MTP. Difficulty stays low.

THE RESULT

If unchanged hash power mines at half difficulty, blocks arrive roughly twice as often. The per-block limit stays unchanged, but the network gets roughly twice as much blockspace per hour.

04 / READING
THE RESULTS

What did the model
actually show?

A model is useful only when its claims stop where its assumptions stop.

THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS

A real capacity mechanism

  • Current timestamp rules can steer difficulty downward.
  • Five-minute blocks can roughly double weight per hour.
  • Reliability rises sharply around 90–95% participation.
  • BIP-54 removes the multi-day boundary reset.
IT DOES NOT YET PROVE

A production-safe scaling policy

  • That miners remain economically willing to cooperate.
  • That network propagation remains safe at every target speed.
  • That applications tolerate distorted Median Time Past.
  • That additional capacity produces additional fee revenue.

06 / CLAIMS UNDER TEST

What sounds right—
but isn't.

The strongest case for preserving the option does not need exaggerated promises.

01“Six confirmations become secure in thirty minutes.”FALSE

At fixed hash rate, doubling block frequency roughly halves expected work per block. Six Warp confirmations contain about three ordinary blocks of work. Inclusion is faster; equal-work settlement is not.

02“Ninety percent is the protocol threshold.”NOT EXACTLY

There is no consensus constant at 90%. In the public-strategy proxy used here, 90% produces a clean epoch about 78% of the time; 95% produces one about 99.45% of the time.

03“A 51% attacker is already fatal, so timewarp adds nothing.”TOO SIMPLE

A majority already has serious powers, but security analysis still distinguishes additional capabilities. Timewarp adds monetary-schedule acceleration and difficulty manipulation to that threat model.

04“More blocks automatically mean more mining fees.”UNPROVEN

Additional capacity can lower the clearing fee or distribute the same transactions across more blocks. Fee revenue rises only if additional demand is strong enough.

05“BIP-54 is only about timewarp.”FALSE

BIP-54 bundles four consensus cleanups. A case against its timestamp rule does not answer its signature-operation, Merkle-tree, or duplicate-transaction proposals.

07 / THE DECISION

What BIP-54 closes.

CURRENT CONSENSUS

A multi-day reset remains possible

t[first] > MTP(previous 11)

The first timestamp of a new difficulty period can fall far behind the previous period's final timestamp, preserving the lag reservoir.

→
BIP-54 PROPOSAL

The reset is limited to two hours

t[N] ≥ t[N−1] − 7200

The required multi-day discontinuity disappears. Material Warp scaling collapses to a residual sub-percent acceleration.

THE OPEN QUESTION

Should ten-minute cadence be an invariant miners cannot alter—or should this option remain until Bitcoin evaluates a safer explicit mechanism for changing L1 capacity?
